# 1-Page Summary: The "Bharat-Realism" Image Eval

## The Eval
**Focus:** Assessing how well AI image models generate culturally authentic, relatable, and realistic lifestyle imagery for Indian e-commerce.
**Use Case:** Lifestyle product shots of Indian clothing in everyday, non-glamorous Indian settings (e.g., a middle-class balcony in Mumbai).

## The Core Setup
**Models Evaluated:**
1. OpenAI — DALL-E 3
2. Google — Gemini 2.5 Flash Image
3. Google — Gemini 3.1 Flash Image Preview

**The Task:** Generate a realistic smartphone photo of an Indian man wearing a simple olive green cotton kurta on a Mumbai apartment balcony at late evening.
**Judging:** 10 participants (18+) blindly rated the outputs on a 1-5 scale across three metrics:
- *Cultural Authenticity* (Are the clothes/setting authentically Indian?)
- *Realism / Candidness* (Does it look like a real smartphone photo, not plastic AI?)
- *Prompt Adherence* (Did it follow the specific instructions?)

## The Main Findings
1. **Gemini 3.1 Flash Image Preview** won overall, particularly excelling in **Realism / Candidness**. It successfully replicated the gritty, natural lighting of a smartphone camera and accurate architectural details of a Mumbai balcony (like standard iron grills).
2. **OpenAI (DALL-E 3)** had the highest **Prompt Adherence** but suffered deeply in Realism. Participants noted the images looked "hyper-saturated," "cartoonish," and "like an expensive studio ad," completely missing the "candid smartphone" requirement.
3. **Gemini 2.5 Flash Image** was a capable middle-ground but struggled slightly with skin textures, making the subject look too airbrushed compared to 3.1.

## The Most Important Takeaway
**Models fail at "ordinary."** While all models can generate a basic Indian face and a kurta, they struggle to generate *candid, unpolished, everyday realism*. For AI to truly disrupt Indian e-commerce advertising, models must be trained to unlearn the default "studio lighting" bias and embrace the nuanced, localized reality of "Bharat."
